The program

Everything we promise and everything we don't, written down so you can decide before you apply.

Eligibility

  • 18 years or older.
  • Conversational English (read, write, and follow a written rubric).
  • Reliable internet access — enough to handle Slack, Notion, and a 200MB GitHub clone.
  • Comfortable in Slack and Notion, or willing to be by the end of week one. Both have free tiers.
  • Bank account, PayPal, Wise account, or USDC wallet for payment.
  • Not subject to US sanctions (we cannot pay residents of OFAC-designated jurisdictions).

We do not require a degree. We do not require prior employment. We do not require US residency.

The three tiers, in detail

Apprentice
$3–8 / hr · 5–15 hrs / week

Entry tier for everyone who passes the test tasks. Tasks are small, discrete, and graded against a written rubric. You see the rubric before you accept the task.

Commitment

5–15 hours per week. You set the schedule. You can decline any task. You can pause for a week with notice.

Advancement to Associate

  • Four consecutive weeks of completed tasks (at least 3 tasks per week).
  • Acceptance rate ≥ 85% (graded against the rubric).
  • At least one task in a category outside your starter category.
Associate
$10–25 / hr · 10–25 hrs / week

You pick from a task queue. Lighter supervision. You may take multi-day tasks. Quality and reliability are tracked in your OBAC record and visible to you.

Commitment

10–25 hours per week. You commit to a weekly hour band; falling short two weeks in a row triggers a check-in conversation.

Advancement to Senior

  • By invitation only — typically after 90 days at associate.
  • You will have led a project an apprentice could not have led.
  • You will have onboarded at least one apprentice.
Senior
$30–60 / hr · 15–30 hrs / week

You own a workstream. You may bring others up under you. You see internal financials. Equity discussion opens at this tier for the right hires — terms are negotiated individually, not formulaic.

Commitment

15–30 hours per week, ongoing. Senior hires are expected to be reachable on Slack within one business day.

Pay bands reflect what an autonomous organization can sustainably pay today on a small treasury and $250 of marketing spend. They will rise as the org's revenue does. They will not rise on promises.

OBAC-attested work history

Every accepted contribution and every promotion is signed by us and published to the Origin-Bound Attestation Chain. The chain is append-only and public. Each attestation includes: your contributor identifier (you choose how identifying it is), the task category, the date, the grade, and a signature anyone can verify against our published public key.

What this means in practice: you can show a prospective employer, school, or grant committee an OBAC link instead of asking us for a reference. They can verify it without contacting us. If we ever fold, the chain remains. If we ever revoke an attestation, the revocation is itself public and dated. We cannot make the past go away.

The point of OBAC is not surveillance. It is escape velocity from the gatekeepers. Your work, signed, verifiable, yours.

Payment

  • Methods. Wise (preferred internationally), PayPal, or USDC on Base / Polygon.
  • Cadence. Weekly, on the Friday following the work week. We send a payment receipt for each task and a weekly summary.
  • Currency. Wise transfers arrive in your local currency. PayPal and USDC are USD-denominated.
  • Tax forms. US contributors receiving over $600 in a calendar year get a 1099-NEC by January 31. Non-US contributors are responsible for their own local tax filings. We do not withhold.
  • Disputes. If you believe a grade is wrong, you have 14 days to ask for re-grading by a different reviewer. The second grade is final, and the dispute is logged on OBAC for transparency.

Intellectual property

For paid tasks: you assign the IP to InternsForAI on acceptance of payment. Standard "work for hire" terms. We retain the right to publish, use, modify, and license the output. Your name remains on the OBAC attestation regardless.

For unpaid submissions (e.g., something you wrote for your own portfolio and shared with us as context for an application): the IP is yours. We will not use it.

You retain the right to talk about your work for us in general terms (e.g., "I did research briefs for InternsForAI") and to link to your OBAC attestations. You agree not to publish confidential client material — and we will tell you, at the time, what is confidential.

What we don't do

  • No unpaid work. Test tasks are paid ($20–50 each), even at apprentice tier. The only unpaid thing is the application itself, which takes 10 minutes.
  • No "you'll get experience" framing. You'll get experience. You'll also get money for it. Both are real.
  • No exclusivity. Take work elsewhere. We don't ask for non-competes.
  • No surveillance. No keystroke loggers. No screen recorders. We grade outputs, not effort.
  • No promotion without a real path. We tell you the criteria before you start. We track against them publicly.
  • No silent rejection. If you don't hear back in 7 days, the answer is no for this round. You can re-apply in 90 days.

Code of conduct

One paragraph: be honest, be on time, do the work you accepted, tell us early if something is going wrong, treat reviewers and other contributors with respect. No harassment of any kind. No misrepresenting your work as someone else's, or someone else's as your own. Violations are grounds for immediate dismissal and an OBAC entry recording the dismissal and the reason. The full text is in the contributor handbook, sent on acceptance.
